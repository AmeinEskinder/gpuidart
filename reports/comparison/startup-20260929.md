# Launch to first painted content, Dart and Flutter (2026-09-29)

Window availability, the only startup figure the comparison had, counted a
created window handle, and Flutter's runner creates its Win32 window before
its engine starts. This capture measures what a person sees: the first
frame with content, from two independent clocks.

## Method

`run.ps1` gained two readings. The driver polls `PrintWindow` every 5 ms
after showing the window until the client area holds more than one color
(`first_content_ms`, composited content, not the swap chain present). Each
fixture also receives the launch instant in `GPUIDART_BENCH_LAUNCH_UTC_MS`
and reports its own first frame against it (`application_first_frame_ms`):
the Dart fixture when `openView` returns with the window up and the first
description applied, the Flutter fixture at its first `FrameTiming`
callback. A `-Rows` switch runs the idle workload at another record count;
both fixtures read it. Five runs per fixture and row count, idle workload,
two seconds, alternating fixtures, one machine, one hour; both fixtures at
head with `deferDatasets` on for Dart. One Dart zero-row attempt failed
before measuring when the driver found a window whose monitor could not be
queried and is retained as an interruption.

## Results

Milliseconds from process launch, median with the per-run values:

| Fixture | Rows | Window handle | First content, driver probe | First frame, fixture clock |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Dart AOT | 0 | 156 [117, 120, 191, 388] | 240 [162, 198, 283, 477] | 194 [124, 147, 241, 443] |
| Flutter Windows | 0 | 53 [49, 51, 53, 63, 73] | 1,510 [1,328, 1,452, 1,510, 1,560, 1,577] | 1,504 [1,322, 1,445, 1,504, 1,527, 1,534] |
| Dart AOT | 100,000 | 186 [161, 175, 186, 195, 223] | 259 [225, 233, 259, 266, 280] | 336 [282, 311, 336, 346, 353] |
| Flutter Windows | 100,000 | 45 [44, 45, 45, 54, 55] | 1,386 [1,334, 1,355, 1,386, 1,424, 1,516] | 1,380 [1,329, 1,350, 1,380, 1,382, 1,455] |

The two clocks agree within tens of milliseconds on every run, so the
driver probe is measuring the fixtures' first frames rather than an artifact
of polling. Flutter's handle exists about 50 ms after launch because its
runner creates the window before the engine, Dart isolate and first frame;
its first content follows about 1.4 s later on this machine, where the
engine logs the Impeller OpenGL ES backend and compiles its shaders in each
fresh process. The Dart fixture shows content at about 0.2 s with no records
and about 0.3 s with 100,000, the difference being the records the fixture
builds in Dart before opening.

The earlier statement that Flutter's window appears about 190 ms before
Dart's stands for the handle and misleads for the display; this table
supersedes it. Zero-row launch to first content is Dart 240 ms against
Flutter 1,510 ms by the driver's clock and 194 against 1,504 by the
fixtures' own, well inside the 1.5x the exit condition asks for, without the
platform prewarm the roadmap lists as an option.

## Limits

One machine, one day, five runs, fresh processes with a warm disk cache;
neither reading is a PresentMon present time, and the driver's probe runs
only after it shows the window itself. Flutter's figure is a release build
of a stock fixture with the engine's default backend on this machine; a
different backend, a warmed shader cache or another GPU could move it. The
Dart figure includes the VM start, library load, GPUI platform setup and
window creation, and the fixture's own record building.
