# Jank during view changes over a million records, Dart and Flutter (2026-09-29)

The [earlier series](view-1m-20260929.md) measured how long a sort, a filter
and a grouping over a million records take. That is throughput, and the
fifth audit pointed out what it hid: the native recompute ran on the GPUI
foreground thread inside the command loop, so for those 150 to 550 ms the
window painted nothing and accepted no input. This series measures what a
user sees instead: while the view changes, how long the longest pause
between painted frames is, and whether a pointer moving over the rows still
produces hover updates.

## What changed

A view over 10,000 records or more now computes off the frame thread
(`bfcbd53`): the publication that changes it is acknowledged at once, the
table keeps its last rows meanwhile, a worker computes the index over a
snapshot of the records, and the result swaps in on the GPUI thread in one
step with the selection and scroll anchor resolved as before. Edits to that
dataset sent in between wait, in order, until the index has landed. A
`table_view` event reports the landed index and `GpuiHost.viewsSettled`
completes when no table is waiting for one.

## Method

`run.ps1`'s `view` workload keeps the pointer moving over the table rows at
60 Hz between its one-per-second clicks, injected through the input queue
as a user's moves would be (`hover_moves` in `run.json`, 713 to 720 per
twelve-second run). Both fixtures repaint for the hover: the Kit table
highlights the hovered row, and the Flutter fixture's rows gained a
`MouseRegion` highlight for parity. A gap between painted frames during a
view change is therefore a stall, not idleness.

Per click the Dart fixture marks native's frame counter in the click
handler (`frame_gaps` diagnostic), times the rebuild acknowledgement
(`apply_us`, now sent before the index computes) and the `table_view`
event that reports the index in place (`settle_us`), and reads the frames
native rendered from the mark to that event with the longest interval
between two of them (`jank.frames_during`, `jank.longest_gap_us`). The
Flutter fixture computes the stage on the UI isolate in the click handler
as before, records the frame number current at the click and at the
post-frame callback that shows the change, and reads the same two figures
from `FrameTiming` between those frames by raster finish, waiting 1.5 s
before saving so the engine's last batch of timings is in
(`changes_with_missing_timings`, zero in every run). The runner verifies
the stage after the clicks and that no record changed, as before.

Three runs per fixture, alternating, 1,000,000 records, twelve seconds
after the warmup, one machine, unattended with the display held awake:
[view-jank-20260929b-0](view-jank-20260929b-0) to [-2](view-jank-20260929b-2).
Every run delivered its twelve clicks with no missed driver deadline and
passed verification. A first attempt an hour earlier
([view-jank-20260929-0](view-jank-20260929-0)) failed activation against
the lock screen: the machine had locked three minutes into its idle time,
which is what taught the harness to hold the display awake.

Acceptance from the audit: zero frames longer than 33 ms during a
million-row sort on the Dart fixture, with the Flutter fixture's longest
gap reported beside it.

## Results

The longest gap between painted frames during a view change, per stage,
over the nine changes of each stage (three runs, three cycles each):

| Stage | Dart longest gap, median [min, max] | Dart frames painted meanwhile, median [min, max] | Flutter longest gap, median [min, max] | Flutter frames meanwhile |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Sort | 18.5 ms [17.6, 33.1] | 8 [6, 10] | 559 ms [509, 724] | 1 |
| Sort + filter | 18.5 ms [17.8, 33.3] | 8 [6, 14] | 622 ms [578, 732] | 1 |
| Sort + filter + group | 19.3 ms [17.9, 35.4] | 23 [17, 26] | 857 ms [756, 1,011] | 1 |
| Back to the records | 0 ms [0, 25.5] | 0 [0, 1] | 21 ms [19, 38] | 1 |

The time from the click handler to the index in place (`settle_us` for
Dart, `frame_us` for Flutter), and what the click handler itself cost:

| Stage | Dart acknowledgement | Dart index in place | Flutter handler compute | Flutter frame shown |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Sort | 0.2 ms [0.1, 1.7] | 136 ms [125, 179] | 537 ms [503, 703] | 541 ms [506, 707] |
| Sort + filter | 0.2 ms [0.1, 0.6] | 139 ms [134, 233] | 601 ms [563, 723] | 602 ms [564, 724] |
| Sort + filter + group | 0.2 ms [0.2, 0.5] | 384 ms [340, 450] | 850 ms [751, 994] | 853 ms [754, 998] |
| Back to the records | 4.9 ms [4.5, 8.1] | 5 ms [5, 8] | 0 ms | 15 ms [5, 18] |

Over all 36 view changes, 6 on the Dart fixture had a gap over 33 ms, the
longest 35.4 ms, and every one of the six sat between 33 and 36 ms: two
frame intervals at 60 Hz, one skipped frame, never more. On the sort stage
alone one change of nine had a 33.1 ms gap. The Flutter fixture had a gap
over 33 ms on 28 of 36 changes, the longest 1,011 ms; on every sort, filter
and group change it painted exactly one frame, the one that shows the
result, because the UI isolate computed the stage for 503 to 994 ms first.

So the audit's line is met to within one frame: no view change on the Dart
fixture stalled the window for more than two frame intervals, against the
130 to 450 ms it would have stalled with the recompute on the frame thread
(the index-in-place times, which are what the synchronous design paid
while painting nothing). Hover updates kept arriving throughout: 6 to 26
frames per change, the grouped stage most because it takes longest.

The index-in-place times are the previous series' throughput figures
under another name: sorting a million permuted prices on the worker
thread takes 125 to 179 ms, grouping them 340 to 450 ms, against 503 to
994 ms for the Flutter fixture's handler; the ratio is 2 to 4 times, as
before, not the order of magnitude the exit table asked for. What changed
is where the time is spent: off the frame thread, behind an
acknowledgement that now returns in 0.2 ms.

Other figures from the runs: the Dart fixture used 34 to 42 percent of one
core and held 656 to 666 MiB of working set (it keeps its Dart copy of the
million records), the Flutter fixture 76 to 81 percent and 455 to 466 MiB.

## Limits

One machine, one day, three runs per fixture. The frame gap is measured at
each fixture's own render or raster step, not at the display; the
input-to-present row still waits on the presentation capture rights. The
Dart frame count comes from the view's render calls, which GPUI may
combine with other work in a frame; the gap between two of them is the
figure to trust. The "back to the records" stage computes in place on
both sides (5 ms on Dart, where the identity index needs no job), so its
gap is idle time between hover frames rather than a stall.
